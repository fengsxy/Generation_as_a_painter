EXPERIMENT 07 · EQUAL STROKE BUDGET

Does coarse-to-fine buy more image per mark?

One target · one canvas size · one mark budget. The experiment measures whether allocating variable-size marks to high-information regions reconstructs the portrait more efficiently than uniform sampling.

Uniform smallDivides the complete image into an even square grid. Every region receives approximately the same number and size of marks.
Fixed coarse-to-fineUses a coarse whole-image pass, then spends predetermined finer marks on the face and identity-critical center. The schedule is written before seeing residuals.
Adaptive coarse-to-fineRecursively splits the region with the greatest color variance × area. Smooth regions retain large marks; complex regions receive smaller marks.
PSNR is computed against the same 320 × 320 target after rendering. A higher value means lower RGB reconstruction error. This experiment tests representation efficiency, not artistic quality or autonomous reasoning. Rectangular marks are used so coverage—not brush texture—is isolated.
